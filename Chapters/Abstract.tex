\chapter*{Abstract}
This thesis is an introduction to Bayesian filtering for state space models, in which a hidden state evolves over time and is observed only through noisy measurements. We develop the Bayesian filtering equations, a two-step recursion of prediction and update that carries a probability distribution over the state forward in time. For linear Gaussian models this recursion has an exact closed-form solution, the Kalman filter. For non-linear models we present the extended Kalman filter as Gaussian approximation and mention the unscented Kalman filter as an alternative. Model parameters such as the noise covariances are estimated by maximum likelihood through the energy function, which the filter computes as a byproduct. The methods are illustrated using simulated tracking problems and then applied to a real price series. In the application, we consider a sequence of increasingly flexible models, beginning with an i.i.d.\ baseline, followed by linear models with time varying mean, and ending with a stochastic volatility model filtered using the extended Kalman filter. We evaluate the predictive performance of these models on a separate test set.
